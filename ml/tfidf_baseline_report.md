## 1. Executive Summary

Phase 6.1 implements the first machine learning baseline for SIH26165. The primary objective is to test whether the raw textual narrative alone (`normalized_narrative`) contains sufficient semantic information to reproduce the frozen V2.3 deterministic rule engine classifications without access to any structured metadata, injury severity indicators, or administrative codes.

### Key Results Summary
* **Pipeline**: Strict Narrative TF-IDF (unigrams + bigrams, 4,886 features) $\rightarrow$ Logistic Regression (`class_weight='balanced'`, $C=100.0$) $\rightarrow$ Locked Decision Threshold ($\tau = 0.59$).
* **Data Split**: Leakage-aware 70/15/15 cluster split (GroupShuffleSplit on `leak_prevention_cluster_id`, random seed 42) with **zero cluster crossover**.
* **Test Performance (Untouched Test Partition, N=147)**:
  * **Accuracy**: **96.60%** (142 / 147 correct)
  * **Macro F1**: **0.9456**
  * **YES Class (SIF Precursor)**: Precision = **0.9915**, Recall = **0.9667**, F1 = **0.9789**
  * **NO Class (Non-SIF Precursor)**: Precision = **0.8667**, Recall = **0.9630**, F1 = **0.9123**
  * **ROC-AUC**: **0.9954**
  * **PR-AUC**: **0.9990**
* **Error Rate**: Only **5 total errors** out of 147 test cases (1 False Positive, 4 False Negatives).

## 5. TF-IDF Representation & Vectorizer Configuration

The TF-IDF vectorizer was **fitted exclusively on the training split** (`X_train = vec.fit_transform(train_df['normalized_narrative'])`). Validation and test splits were transformed strictly via `transform()`.

### Vectorizer Specification
* `ngram_range`: `(1, 2)` (captures key phrases such as `"high voltage"`, `"conveyor belt"`, `"slipped fell"`, `"hand tool"`)
* `min_df`: `2` (removes unique typographical artifacts and singleton noise)
* `max_features`: `5000`
* `sublinear_tf`: `True` ($1 + \log(\text{tf})$, prevents high-frequency token dominance)
* `strip_accents`: `'unicode'`
* `lowercase`: `True`

### Vocabulary Metrics
* **Final Vocabulary Size**: **4,886 features**
* **Training Non-Zero Entries**: **40,590 elements**
* **Feature Matrix Sparsity**: **98.78%**

## 9. Error Analysis & Failure Archetypes

Across 147 test cases, exactly **5 classification errors** occurred (1 False Positive, 4 False Negatives). All 5 records were audited against the 10 failure archetypes.

### Inventory of All Test Set Misclassifications

| Candidate ID | True V2.3 Label | Pred Label | Model Score | V2.3 Reason Code | Failure Archetype | Summary of Narrative |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `SIR_1065535` | **NO** | **YES** | 0.7007 | `LOW_ENERGY_MANUAL_TOOL` | Industrial vocabulary in low-risk contexts vs. machine entrapment | Using wrench to clear snow from conveyor belt; wrench caught; trapped under conveyor belt bar. |
| `SIR_1130964` | **YES** | **NO** | 0.0447 | `GRAVITATIONAL_EXPOSURE` | Low-height falls ($\le 4\text{ ft}$) with ambiguous machinery elevation | Exiting crane, walked on tracks, slipped and fell two feet off tracks, fractured leg. |
| `SIR_963496` | **YES** | **NO** | 0.4447 | `GRAVITATIONAL_EXPOSURE` | Same-level slips/trips carrying heavy materials | Slipped and fell on ice while carrying a 30-foot pipe, fractured tibia/fibula. |
| `HSE_220850069_3523`| **YES** | **NO** | 0.5008 | `ELECTRICAL_CONTACT` | Electrical exposure diluted by verbose non-industrial narrative | Holding energized 480V cord while walking to stairs; electrocuted upon touching metal railing. |
| `SIR_974353` | **YES** | **NO** | 0.5109 | `GRAVITATIONAL_EXPOSURE` | Unconventional wording & non-industrial settings | Decorating church sanctuary for Easter; tripped on steps to platform, broke hip. |

---

### In-Depth Archetype Breakdown

#### 1. Industrial Vocabulary in Low-Risk Contexts (False Positive: `SIR_1065535`, Score = 0.7007)
* **Narrative**: *"An employee was using a wrench to remove snow that was blocking the magic carpet conveyor belt machine. As soon as the snow was released, the conveyor started going again and grabbed the wrench. When the employee tried to grab the tool, she was trapped under the conveyor belt bar, breaking her right humerus."*
* **Root Cause**: The linear model responded strongly to prominent industrial bigrams (`"conveyor belt"`, `"conveyor started"`, `"belt machine"`, `"trapped under"`), predicting YES with 0.7007 confidence. In contrast, the V2.3 rule engine fired the manual tool precedence rule (`LOW_ENERGY_MANUAL_TOOL`) because the initiating mechanism was clearing snow with a hand tool (`"wrench"`).
* **Takeaway**: Linear n-gram models cannot resolve hierarchical tool-vs-machine precedence without syntactic or dependency parsing.

#### 2. Low-Height Falls ($\le 4\text{ ft}$) (False Negative: `SIR_1130964`, Score = 0.0447)
* **Narrative**: *"An employee exiting a crane was walking on the tracks and slipped and fell two feet off the tracks breaking the right leg."*
* **Root Cause**: The model assigned a very low score (0.0447) because `"fell two feet"` is strongly associated across training data with non-SIF same-level or low-height falls. However, V2.3 fired `GRAVITATIONAL_EXPOSURE` because crane tracks/superstructures satisfy the heavy equipment elevation rule.
* **Takeaway**: Bag-of-words models struggle when a metric phrase like `"two feet"` contradicts the contextual elevation of a crane superstructure.

#### 3. Same-Level Slips/Trips with Heavy Loads (False Negative: `SIR_963496`, Score = 0.4447)
* **Narrative**: *"On or before February 19, 2015, an employee slipped and fell on ice while carrying a 2-inch x 30-foot-long piece of pipe, resulting in a tibia/fibula fracture to the right leg."*
* **Root Cause**: The dominant n-grams (`"slipped and fell"`, `"on ice"`, `"fracture to"`) are heavily weighted toward NO / same-level slips. The SIF trigger—carrying a massive 30-foot industrial pipe—was diluted by the generic slip-and-fall vocabulary, yielding a score of 0.4447 (below the 0.59 threshold).

#### 4. Electrical Exposure Diluted by Verbose Narrative (False Negative: `HSE_220850069_3523`, Score = 0.5008)
* **Narrative**: *"At 2:45 p.m. on May 3, 2016, an employee was apparently walking while holding onto a 480 volt SO Cord that was energized. He had partially disassembled the plug prior to this through the use of an allen wrench and screw driver. There were no witnesses. The coroner responded to the scene following the accident. The coroner determined that the employee was walking towards a fixed flight of stairs. The employee reached for the metal stair railing with his left hand while holding on to the energized, flexible cord with his right. The employee was electrocuted and killed. Burn marks were found on the palms of both hands."*
* **Root Cause**: The narrative is long (92 words) and contains extensive forensic/procedural terms (`"apparently walking"`, `"prior to this"`, `"coroner responded"`, `"stair railing"`). While `"480 volt"` and `"electrocuted"` pushed the score up to 0.5008, the procedural padding kept it just below the 0.59 threshold. Under default $\tau=0.50$, this case would have been correctly predicted as YES.

#### 5. Unconventional Wording & Non-Industrial Environments (False Negative: `SIR_974353`, Score = 0.5109)
* **Narrative**: *"An employee was decorating the church sanctuary for Easter. He was going up the steps to the platform when he tripped and fell backwards, breaking his hip."*
* **Root Cause**: Non-industrial tokens (`"church sanctuary"`, `"Easter"`, `"decorating"`) do not appear in industrial hazard contexts, suppressing positive weights. The score landed at 0.5109, falling short of the locked 0.59 threshold.

## 11. Conclusion & Next Steps

The Phase 6.1 TF-IDF + Logistic Regression baseline demonstrates that **raw incident narratives contain strong textual signals that agree with the frozen V2.3 deterministic rule engine with 96.60% accuracy and a 0.9456 Macro F1 score**.

The model demonstrates:
1. **Near-zero false alarms**: 99.15% precision on SIF precursors (only 1 false positive out of 117 positive predictions).
2. **High sensitivity**: 96.67% recall on genuine SIF precursors.
3. **Failure bounds**: The few errors stem from linear bag-of-words limitations—namely, hierarchical tool-vs-machine precedence (`SIR_1065535`), metric vs contextual height nuances (`SIR_1130964`), and narrative dilution in verbose prose (`HSE_220850069_3523`).

Phase 6.1 is formally concluded. No further tuning or modifications have been performed. Ready for Phase 6.2 upon instruction.
